Key Stories
#

The Jobs Report – Still Not Great: The 98,000 jobs added in June, as reported by ADP, represents a continuation of the churn we’ve been observing over the past several months. While improved from May, it marks the smallest increase in three months and highlights the inherent weakness in underlying demand for labor. This lends further credence to the concerns around sluggish consumer spending and poses questions about whether businesses are truly ramping up investment or simply adjusting to a post-pandemic world. The market has largely priced in a slowing economy, but this data serves as a gut-check moment – is it time to reevaluate those assumptions? Investors should watch closely for revisions to previous months’ figures; the ADP report isn’t perfect, but provides a valuable early indicator of what Friday’s official jobs number might hold.

Semiconductor Volatility Surges: The resurgence in volatility for chip stocks is arguably the most concerning development this morning. The run-up in many names has been nothing short of spectacular over the past year, fuelled by AI mania. But as Nomura points out in their analysis, there are still overlooked bottlenecks and hyperscalers forced to keep spending—suggesting sustained demand even amidst the current volatility—and that’s what should attract long-term investors, but today, sentiment is leaning toward risk aversion. A sharp rise in VIX could trigger a broader selloff, especially if we see further disappointing earnings reports or negative news regarding supply chain constraints. This isn’t necessarily time to abandon the sector entirely; it may present an opportunity to buy the dip for patient investors—but only after thorough due diligence and a clear understanding of the risks.

Warsh’s Warning & Rate Hikes: Kevin Warsh’s dismissal of forward guidance is significant, signaling a potential shift in the Federal Reserve’s communication strategy—and one that could lead to increased market volatility. While transparency can be beneficial, Warsh argues it can create distortions and undermine the Fed’s credibility. The implications are clear: expect fewer clues about future rate movements, which will force investors to rely more on economic data and intuition. With inflation remaining stubbornly elevated, the possibility of further rate hikes – however unpopular—remains very much alive. The market is scrutinizing every piece of economic information with a fine-toothed comb, searching for hints about the Fed’s next move.

Today’s Takeaway
#

Don’t be fooled by superficial market rallies. While AI remains a bright spot, today’s news reinforces the message: the economy is at a crossroads, and investors must adopt a more disciplined, risk-aware approach. Expect continued volatility as both economic data and Fed communication create uncertainty—and remember, even in a frothy environment, capital preservation should always be paramount.
