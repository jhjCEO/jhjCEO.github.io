Key Stories

The Iran Deal: A Moment of Grace? The news of a peace deal with Iran, culminating in the reopening of the Strait of Hormuz, provided a momentary balm to frayed nerves. The oil shock we saw earlier this year, which threatened to choke off global growth, seems, for now, to have been averted. Investors piled into energy stocks briefly, but the gains quickly unwound. While the geopolitical risk factor has undeniably been reduced, let’s not mistake a temporary reprieve for a permanent solution. The Middle East remains a powder keg, and the underlying tensions haven’t magically vanished. Furthermore, the market has likely priced in a degree of detente, meaning the upside from this news is likely limited. The real test will be how the deal holds up over the coming weeks and months—any hint of backsliding could trigger a swift correction. We’re still not completely out of the woods.

Warsh’s Shadow: Fed Meeting Looms Large. All eyes are now on the Federal Reserve and its new chair, Kevin Warsh. Economists are openly admitting they have no idea what his stance will be, creating a considerable amount of anxiety. The market needs clarity on the Fed’s trajectory—will they continue down the path of rate hikes, or will they signal a pause or even a pivot? This ambiguity is weighing heavily on sentiment, and the upcoming press conference will be crucial. Should Warsh signal a hawkish approach, expect a sharp sell-off in growth stocks and a rise in fixed-income yields. Conversely, a dovish tone could send risk assets soaring Today's Voca soaring[sɔːr]Defense stocks soared after the contract award. (계약 수주 이후 방산주가 급등했습니다.) . It’s a line in the sand moment for the market. Positioning has become increasingly complex – many are on the sidelines, hedging their bets until Warsh speaks.

The Fixed Income Play: Pimco’s warning about defaults and their advice to increasingly seek out fixed income is resonating. Today’s Takeaway

Don’t chase the initial pop from the Iran deal. The market is telling us that geopolitical stability alone isn’t enough to sustain a rally when the Fed’s actions—and the potential for higher rates—remain the dominant narrative. It’s time to play it safe and prioritize risk management.
